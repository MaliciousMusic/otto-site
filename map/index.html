The map of objectives, tests and results

Roots at the bottom, the goal at the top. A smile means measured, a round mouth means we are asking now, closed eyes mean not started.

Inside the graph, use the arrow keys to move between cells and Enter to open one. The list view has the same content.

Pick anything

Each cell is an objective. Dishes are tests. Drops are results. Pull a cell and let go: it knows its place.

Arrows are candidate dependencies. “Part of the definition” says what a word means, and never that the ability exists. One dependency out of 38 is supported by a test; one is refuted.

Objectives, from the roots up

  1. Minimal world

    Measured, within a bounded domain

    Local transitions on a finite state, visible and replayable.

    May lead to: Persistence & response (supported), Resources (plausible), Boundary (plausible), Causal memory (plausible), Heredity (plausible).

  2. Scale

    Not started, no evidence yet

    How effects, costs and limits move with size, resources and time.

    May lead to: Self-change (optional) (contested or unknown), Otto speaks and understands by itself (plausible).

  3. Persistence & response

    Measured, within a bounded domain

    A pattern lasts over a declared time and responds to a declared disturbance.

    May lead to: Organisation (contested or unknown), Otto speaks and understands by itself (refuted).

  4. Boundary

    We are working here

    A unit keeps its causal identity while its parts are replaced.

    May lead to: Organisation (plausible).

  5. Resources

    We are working here

    Limited flows change what can happen and what can be maintained.

    May lead to: Organisation (plausible).

  6. Causal memory

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A trace of the past changes a later response.

    May lead to: Plasticity (plausible), Adaptation (plausible), Communication (plausible), Seeking information (plausible).

  7. Plasticity

    Not started, no evidence yet

    Interaction changes a rule or a link, and the change lasts.

    May lead to: Adaptation (plausible).

  8. Organisation

    We are working here

    Activities help maintain the very conditions that make them possible.

    May lead to: Adaptation (contested or unknown).

  9. Heredity

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A variation survives the replacement of the units that carry it.

    May lead to: Evolution (part of the definition).

  10. Seeking information

    Not started, no evidence yet

    An action changes what can be seen, and that changes a later decision.

    May lead to: Asking (plausible), Its own research (plausible).

  11. Adaptation

    Not started, no evidence yet

    The response to a context changes after interaction, with a bounded causal benefit.

    May lead to: Evolution (contested or unknown).

  12. Evolution

    Not started, no evidence yet

    Transmitted variants change in frequency because they spread differently.

    May lead to: New individuals (contested or unknown), Accumulation (contested or unknown).

  13. Communication

    Not started, no evidence yet

    An emission changes what another system does next. Not words yet: a signal that matters.

    May lead to: Accumulation (plausible), Asking (contested or unknown).

  14. Accumulation

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A lineage keeps and combines changes beyond what it would rediscover alone.

    May lead to: Asking (contested or unknown), Otto speaks and understands by itself (part of the definition).

  15. New individuals

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A group starts to transmit and depend on itself at its own level.

    May lead to: Accumulation (contested or unknown).

  16. Asking

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A distinction between possibilities decides which experiment comes next.

    May lead to: Causal prediction (plausible).

  17. Causal prediction

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A retained structure predicts what an unseen intervention will do.

    May lead to: Accumulation (contested or unknown), Understanding (plausible).

  18. Understanding

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A relation keeps working after its surface, its place or its context has changed.

    May lead to: Its own research (plausible), Otto speaks and understands by itself (part of the definition).

  19. Its own research

    Not started, no evidence yet

    Otto proposes, intervenes, reads the result and revises, with nobody choosing for it.

    May lead to: Self-change (optional) (contested or unknown), Otto speaks and understands by itself (part of the definition).

  20. Self-change (optional)

    Not started, no evidence yet

    A change Otto makes improves its ability to make further useful changes.

    May lead to: Otto speaks and understands by itself (contested or unknown).

  21. Otto speaks and understands by itself

    Not started, no evidence yet

    Autonomous, cumulative intelligence: gains in inquiry and transfer, kept and reused across contexts.

    Nothing lies above it on the map.

Tests

  1. First run

    Holds, within its domain: replays

    One world of 32 cells, 128 steps. Can it be replayed exactly? Yes. Events, measures and summary match byte for byte. One seed only, so it says nothing general.

    Bears on: Minimal world.

  2. Law 25%

    Holds, within its domain: 64/64

    Does the law do anything compared with a frozen world? Sparse start. Fewer groups in all 64 pairs. Mean change −4.27 (95% interval −4.67 to −3.86).

    Bears on: Minimal world.

  3. Law 50%

    Holds, within its domain: 64/64

    Same question, balanced start. Fewer groups in all 64 pairs. Mean change −5.45 (95% interval −5.92 to −4.98).

    Bears on: Minimal world.

  4. All at once?

    Holds, within its domain: 49/64

    Does it matter if cells update together or one by one? Yes. 49 of 64 matched starts end elsewhere (95% interval 65% to 85%).

    Bears on: Minimal world.

  5. Four orders

    Holds, within its domain: 56/64

    Same start, four random orders. One ending or several? Several for 56 of 64 starts. 2.67 endings on average, a lower bound.

    Bears on: Minimal world.

  6. One poke

    Holds, within its domain: 35/64

    Flip one cell of a settled world. Does it come back? 35 of 64 end elsewhere, 29 are restored exactly. The link with borders was noticed after the fact, so it was retested.

    Bears on: Persistence & response.

  7. Poke 32 cells

    Holds, within its domain: 64·64·0

    Registered in advance: does the distance to a border decide? At distance 0 and 1, all 64 change. At distance 2 or more, none does.

    Bears on: Persistence & response.

  8. Poke 64 cells

    Holds, within its domain: 64·64·0

    Does it still hold in a world twice as large? Same split on 64 new starts. One size transfer, nothing more.

    Bears on: Persistence & response, Scale.

  9. Two-bit loop

    Toy model only: 8 cases

    In a tiny loop, does cutting an inner link stop what it maintains? Yes, at step 9, and replacing the role keeps it. But we wrote the rule, so this is a check of the fixture.

    Bears on: Organisation.

  10. Renewal measure

    Rejected: 38/128

    Can a measure tell real self-renewal from look-alikes, whoever is looking? All look-alikes were caught. But 38 of 128 paths changed verdict with the observer, so we rejected our own measure.

    Bears on: Organisation.

  11. Cut & replace

    Not run: blocked

    Which inner part really keeps the whole going, once every outside help is matched? Not run. It stays blocked until a measurement survives.

    Bears on: Organisation.
