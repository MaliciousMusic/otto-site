Questions

The answer first, then the reason.

What is Otto?

Otto is an artificial life research project. It looks for the smallest set of rules and constraints from which something could organise itself, then learn, communicate and understand, without any of those abilities being programmed in. Today it is a small, fully replayable computer world, a record of tests, and a map of what still has to be shown.

Is Otto an AI or a chatbot?

No. There is no language model, no training data, no reward and no goal inside Otto. The current world is a ring of cells that each copy the majority around them. The aim is to find out what such a world needs before anything like intelligence could appear by itself.

Can Otto speak or understand?

Not today. Speaking and understanding by itself is the goal at the top of the map. Two of 21 objectives, the minimal world and its persistence, have been measured, each in a small domain. Communication, questioning and understanding have no evidence yet.

What counts as emergence here?

A new ability counts only if it was not programmed, was not hidden in the world, was not supplied by an outside helper, can be reproduced, carries over to new conditions, and has an identified cause.

Why start with such a simple world?

Because a simple world cannot hide an answer. The majority rule is well known, so it serves as a calibration: it shows that the engine, the measurements and the replays can be trusted before anything harder is tried.

What has been measured so far?

The rule gathers cells into fewer groups in 64 of 64 matched worlds, at two densities. The order of updates alone changes the ending in 56 of 64 worlds. A single poke is repaired exactly when it lands two or more cells from a border, and changes the ending otherwise, at 32 and 64 cells. A two-bit loop behaved as written. One measure of self-renewal was rejected.

Why publish a rejected measure?

Because it is a result. The measure caught all 198 look-alike paths, but 38 of 128 candidate paths changed verdict when the observer looked at another time or with another step. The rule, fixed before the run, was that a verdict must not depend on the observer, so the measure was dropped and the next test stays blocked.

How is this different from training a neural network?

Training pushes a system toward a score chosen by people. Otto has no score. Measurements look at the world from outside and never feed back into it, so nothing can drift toward what the researcher hopes to see.

Are the results reproducible?

The 1,090 archived runs of the ring world replay byte for byte. The 327 paths of the renewal study do so only in the environment that recorded them: under another Python version, 198 of them differ by at most 7.1e-15, and none of the 2,943 verdicts changes. These are replays by the same code. No independent implementation has reproduced them yet, and the record says so. The small engine on this site is a separate port of the ring world that lands where 769 recorded runs landed.

Is Otto alive or conscious?

Nothing in the record supports that, and the project does not claim it.

What happens next?

Deciding what it means for a system to maintain a condition over time, in a way that does not depend on when the observer looks. No new run starts before that is settled.

Who is behind Otto?

Otto is solo research by Hadrien Lonjaret, started in 2026.

Is the code open?

The research code is private for now and no licence has been chosen. The recorded results shown on this site can be downloaded as JSON and CSV from the record page.

Why does serious research look this cute?

So that people stay long enough to see how careful it is. The faces carry meaning too: a smile means measured, a round mouth means a question in progress, closed eyes mean not started.

Glossary

Artificial life
The study of life-like processes by building them, here in a computer.
Cell
One place in the world. It holds a single value, shown as green or blue.
Ring
The shape of the world: a circle of cells, so that every cell has two neighbours.
Majority law
The rule of the current world. At each step a cell takes the value most common among itself and its two neighbours. Also known as elementary rule 232.
Seed
The number that fixes the starting world. The same seed always gives the same world.
Density
The chance that a cell starts green.
Group
A run of neighbouring green cells. Called an occupied cluster in the record.
Fixed point
A world that the rule no longer changes.
Border
A cell whose two neighbours differ. Called an interface in the record.
Poke
Changing one cell by hand in a settled world. Called a single-site flip in the record.
Update order
Whether all cells change together (synchronous) or one after another in a random order (sequential).
Replay
Running the same code again from the same seed and comparing every byte. A replay is not an independent replication.
Preregistration
Writing down and locking what will be measured, and what would prove it wrong, before the run.
Observer
The part that measures. It reads the world and never changes it.
Look-alike
Something that could be mistaken for the real thing, such as a wave, a crystal or a pattern fed from outside.
Organisation
Activities that help maintain the very conditions that make them possible. Not shown in Otto yet.
Ablation
Removing one part to see what depended on it.
Confidence interval
The range of values compatible with the data. Proportions here use the Wilson 95% interval.
